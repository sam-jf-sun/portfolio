Hi,

Happy you're here!

From a young age, I've been driven by a constant need to explore and master. My school years were filled with creative pursuits like violin and orchestra and competitive activities such as squash and basketball. I was always looking for something new to learn and challenge me.

As a professional architect, I focused on the rigorous process of designing, planning and executing high-rise structures. I enjoyed translating complex blueprints into tangible, functional structures. But eventually, I felt the familiar itch for a new challenge.

My attention pivoted to the tech industry, prompting a fundamental question: "Is building a mobile app like designing a physical space? Do they share the same strategic DNA of planning and user-focused execution?" Driven by this, I began my transition into UX/UI design.

Now, I leverage my architectural foundation to solve meaningful problems, while continuing to refine my craft with the same passion that first drew me to it.

How I use AI currently

AI is embedded across almost every phase of my process now, not as a novelty but as a part of how I work. The shift is that AI has compressed the time between having an idea and having something tangible to react to.

01 Discovery & research

I use Notion AI to transcribe user interviews and stakeholder meetings in real time

02 Problem Framing & Strategy

I use Claude for the thinking-heavy lifting like sharpening problem statements, stress-testing design rationale and even pushing back on my assumptions

03 Ideation & Concept Generation

Before I touch Figma, I'll use Claude or Gemini to rapidly generate a range of conceptual directions in plain language

04 Visual Exploration

I use Whisk to generate reference imagery that's closer to what I'm actually envisioning

05 Production in Figma

I use Figma Make to move from rough concepts to testable prototypes much faster, particularly for component variants and responsive layout exploration
