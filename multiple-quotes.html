Project Overview

The KINTO model allows customers to borrow vehicles while paying fixed monthly installments over a set period. The multiple quotes feature enables customers to select from curated quote options displaying different vehicle models, accessories, and discounts for informed decision-making.

Problem Statement

Sales consultants creating KINTO quotes struggle to efficiently present multiple options to customers due to its repetitive, time-consuming and manual process. Starting each quote from scratch with only minor variations diverts focus from selling to admin work, causing frustration and reducing productivity.

01

The Team

Arriving at Toyota, I found design was treated as a novelty rather than standard practice. I led building design infrastructure from zero while pioneering internal processes and educating cross-functional partners on design thinking methodologies.

The Toyota design team
02

A Unified Digital Identity

The initial phase focused on architecting a comprehensive design system for the E-Toyota One platform. I built a scalable component library establishing a unified visual language, applying atomic design principles to ensure consistent integration across the platform ecosystem.

E-Toyota design system component library
03

Conceptualising the Price Comparison Tool

I recognised dealer demand for simultaneous quote comparisons. I conducted dealer visits and focus groups to obtain insights that would reshape a platform previously limited to single quote creation.

04

Prototype-first Approach

Given the complexity and Toyota's unfamiliarity with Figma, I adopted a prototype-first methodology, helping stakeholders understand ideas and the value of collaborative design tools.

05

From Concept to Reality

I collaborated with business analysts, product owners, and developers on iterative mock-ups, breaking prototypes into distinct user stories using Agile methodologies. At times I juggled dual roles as designer and analyst when business analysts were unavailable.

06

Desk Check Alignment

I conducted regular desk-checks with developers to verify alignment between their work and the prototype. I carefully versioned Figma screens and prototypes, keeping developers focused on approved components.

07

Training

I conducted multiple workshops with KINTO dealers before release, introducing the new feature. Overall feedback sentiment proved positive.

08

Reflecting on the Journey

Toyota's minimal design methodology exposure created a challenge — the absence of initial user research including competitive analysis and persona development. My future aspiration is to advocate these approaches to enhance the value proposition.
